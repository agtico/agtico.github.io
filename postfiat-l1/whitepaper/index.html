    PostFiat L1 Whitepaper¶

    Status: canonical unified whitepaper Date: 2026-05-19 Scope: controlled testnet implementation and public-launch design

    PostFiat is an XRP-style authority-validator settlement chain rebuilt for the constraints that now matter: post-quantum authorization, private settlement, Cobalt validator governance, fixed supply, fee burn, no native validator subsidy, and a memory-safe Rust implementation.

    The premise is simple. XRP proved that a known-validator settlement network can carry large economic value without proof-of-work mining rewards or proof-of-stake reward markets. PostFiat keeps that useful settlement model and rebuilds the parts that are difficult to retrofit into an older network: quantum-resistant account and validator signatures, real shielded settlement paths, explicit validator trust evolution, modern operator evidence, and a code base designed around deterministic Rust protocol execution.

    This paper replaces the earlier split between the technical paper, the mathematics paper, and the "why a new XRP-style chain" paper. The old drafts are archived under docs/archive/whitepaper-drafts/2026-05-19/.

    What XRP Proved¶

    XRP proved a category:

    • fast deterministic settlement can run with known validators;
    • a chain can operate without mining rewards;
    • a chain can operate without native staking rewards;
    • fee burn can price spam without becoming a validator compensation system;
    • fixed supply can be part of the monetary promise;
    • a low-cost validator network can still matter economically.

    That category is different from Bitcoin and different from proof of stake. Bitcoin pays miners because proof of work converts energy and hardware cost into settlement security. Proof of stake pays validators or gives them yield because stake is the control surface. XRP uses a different assumption: validators can be natural stakeholders in the settlement system. Exchanges, custodians, market makers, issuers, allocators, infrastructure providers, and large users can have a direct business reason to keep the network reliable.

    PostFiat starts from that category instead of pretending every settlement chain needs an inflation-funded validator class.

    Why A New Chain Exists¶

    The reason to build a new XRP-style chain is not that the old model was useless. The reason is that the old model was incomplete for the next version of institutional crypto settlement.

    The missing pieces are:

    • post-quantum authorization from genesis, instead of long-lived classical keys and a later emergency migration;
    • private settlement for buy-side and capital-markets workflows;
    • validator governance that is explicit, signed, replayable, and part of chain state;
    • bounded public RPC and wallet tooling that institutions can inspect;
    • current-state and partial-history validation so validators do not have to retain every byte forever;
    • Rust implementation discipline instead of inheriting a large C++ fork whose assumptions were not written for this design.

    PostFiat is not a clone. It is a rebuild of the authority-validator settlement idea around those requirements.

    Design Summary¶

    PostFiat has six core design decisions.

    1. Proof of authority. Validators are known infrastructure operators and natural stakeholders, not anonymous miners or reward farmers.
    2. Privacy. Confidential settlement is a base workflow for institutional finance, not a cosmetic add-on.
    3. Cobalt governance. Validator trust evolution is handled through explicit Cobalt-derived governance mechanics rather than informal trust-list history.
    4. Fixed supply. The native unit has a 100 billion genesis supply and no native inflation schedule.
    5. No native validator rewards. Fees price resource use and are burned; they do not become validator yield.
    6. Quantum-resistant authorization. The base transparent account and validator paths use ML-DSA-style signatures and accept the size and bandwidth costs.

    These decisions are connected. A fixed-supply chain with no native validator subsidy needs low operating costs. A low-cost authority-validator chain needs credible validator governance. A settlement chain for buy-side workflows needs privacy. A chain built now should not start with cryptography that already has a known long-horizon migration problem.

    Monetary Model¶

    The native supply is fixed at 100 billion units.

    PostFiat's monetary rule is:

    genesis_supply = 100_000_000_000
native_issuance_per_block = 0
validator_reward_per_block = 0
supply_next = supply_current - fees_burned

    Fees exist to price spam, state growth, verifier work, and network resources. They are not a mining reward and not a staking yield. The system does not need to create a paid native validator class to operate the base chain.

    This is the same broad monetary intuition that made fixed-supply crypto legible in the first place: holders should not need to model a future monetary committee deciding how much dilution is necessary to pay infrastructure. If validators are natural stakeholders and the cost of validation is kept low, the chain can buy reliability through alignment rather than permanent issuance.

    Validator Model¶

    PostFiat uses proof of authority in the literal sense: validators are known participants whose keys, manifests, and trust relationships are part of the network's operating state.

    The validator job is to:

    • verify transactions and batches;
    • vote on ordered blocks;
    • publish certificates and receipts;
    • serve read RPC;
    • retain the history needed for their configured role;
    • participate in Cobalt governance for validator-set and amendment evolution.

    The validator does not earn a native block reward. The validator's incentive is external to protocol issuance: reliable settlement is useful to the operator. That is a cleaner fit for institutional settlement than a system whose validator economics depend on inflation, reward farming, or MEV extraction.

    Cobalt Governance¶

    XRP's Unique Node List model solved an important problem: validators do not all need to be anonymous and permissionless for a settlement network to work. The remaining problem is validator trust evolution. A professional settlement network should not depend on informal off-chain trust-list history, ad hoc social coordination, or opaque validator-list updates.

    Cobalt was the Ripple-funded XRPL research answer to this problem. Its core idea is that participants can have local trust views, those views can overlap through essential subsets and thresholds, and the network can still ratify governance amendments through Byzantine agreement when the trust graph is linked correctly.

    PostFiat separates governance from fast transaction ordering:

    • ordinary transactions use the fast certified ordering path;
    • Cobalt governs validators, trust graphs, registry roots, amendments, and protocol transitions.

    That separation matters. The chain does not need to run the full Cobalt governance path for every payment. It needs Cobalt where trust changes.

    What exists now:

    • non-identical trust views;
    • essential subsets with t_S and q_S threshold parameters;
    • linkedness checking before trust graph activation;
    • fail-closed rejection of unsafe trust graphs;
    • non-uniform governance certificates;
    • reliable broadcast, binary agreement, multi-valued agreement, and democratic atomic broadcast mechanics for amendments;
    • validator registry and trust graph transitions ratified through Cobalt;
    • stale replay rejection across activated trust graphs;
    • deterministic adversarial packets for collusion thresholds, captured groups, trust-graph poisoning, RBC/ABBA equivocation, MVBA/DABC invalid candidates, membership races, partitions, crash/restart, resource DoS, governance spam, amendment replay bundles, parser/canonical-payload fuzzing, and long adversarial soak.

    The controlled-testnet Cobalt mechanics are built and machine-checked. Current evidence runs seven logical validators under project-controlled infrastructure. Public launch adds stronger placement evidence: more independent host, operator, legal-domain, and funding-domain separation. That is a topology and public-credibility requirement, not a reason to block the controlled-testnet code path.

    Transaction Ordering And Finality¶

    PostFiat orders ordinary transactions through a fast BFT-style certified batch path. Validators vote on proposed blocks, certificates bind the quorum evidence, and receipts let clients verify finality.

    The latency problem found during launch hardening was not Rust and not quantum signatures by themselves. It was harness and hot-path structure: serial vote collection, serial certified-batch broadcast, and repeated full block replay on the finality path. The current implementation parallelizes vote collection and certified broadcast, adds hot-path transaction-finality lookup, and keeps full replay as an audit path.

    Current controlled evidence:

    • local 5-validator 20-round finality: submit_to_finality p50 1.563s, p95 1.709s, p99 1.753s;
    • local certified round p50 0.921s, p95 1.043s;
    • local tx finality RPC p50 0.062s, p95 0.068s;
    • remote 5-validator normal-run peer-certified round p50 1.032s, p95 1.116s, p99 1.139s.

    Quantum-Resistant Authorization¶

    PostFiat treats quantum risk as an engineering input, not a slogan.

    The base transparent account and validator authorization paths use ML-DSA-style signatures. That choice has costs:

    • signatures are larger;
    • validator certificates are larger;
    • bandwidth and storage pressure are higher;
    • wallets and custodians cannot simply copy BIP32/ECDSA assumptions.

    Those costs are acceptable for this design because a greenfield settlement chain can price them from the start. Retrofitting post-quantum authorization after a network has years of classical long-lived keys is a harder problem.

    What exists now:

    • post-quantum account signing flows and wallet vectors;
    • post-quantum validator transport envelopes;
    • certificate handling that accepts larger signature material;
    • ML-DSA performance and size evidence;
    • RPC and SDK paths that sign, submit, and confirm transparent transfers.

    Privacy And Confidential Settlement¶

    Transparent ledgers are useful for public audit, but they are a bad default for many buy-side and capital-markets workflows. Public settlement leaks balances, counterparties, timing, position changes, treasury movement, and execution intent. A chain aimed at institutional settlement needs confidential transfers, selective disclosure, and public accounting boundaries that do not reveal every workflow in cleartext.

    PostFiat uses an Orchard/Halo2 privacy path for shielded value. The design uses notes, commitments, nullifiers, retained roots, encrypted outputs, turnstile accounting, and selective disclosure packets. Transparent funds can enter the Orchard pool, move privately, and withdraw back to transparent accounts through bounded signed envelopes.

    What exists now:

    • Orchard/Halo2 adapter using the Rust/Zcash Orchard stack;
    • direct transparent-to-Orchard deposit envelopes;
    • one-note Orchard spend construction;
    • one-note Orchard withdraw construction;
    • nullifier persistence and duplicate-spend rejection;
    • retained roots and scan material for local wallet spending;
    • orchard-scan, orchard-disclose, and orchard-disclosure-verify;
    • public Orchard pool telemetry through orchard_pool_report;
    • bounded RPC batch creation for Orchard actions and direct deposits;
    • public-edge rejection of raw private witness/key fields;
    • malformed-proof, rate-limit, child-isolation, and concurrency-limit evidence;
    • live five-validator direct deposit, spend, withdraw, outage, restart, snapshot, soak, and audit-packet evidence.

    The current privacy path is a controlled privacy implementation being hardened for semi-production use. Production launch work remains in wallet security, custodian flows, compliance policy, longer mixed soaks, external cryptographic review, implementation audit, and post-quantum note-encryption migration.

    Rust Implementation Rationale¶

    PostFiat is written in Rust because the protocol needs deterministic execution, bounded parsing, explicit serialization, safe key handling boundaries, and operator software that is difficult to crash accidentally.

    Rust is not magic. It is still possible to write bad protocol code in Rust. But for this design it reduces a large class of memory-safety risk that a C++ fork would inherit. It also lets the implementation organize consensus, storage, networking, RPC, privacy, and cryptography behind typed interfaces instead of threading new assumptions through a codebase built for a different era.

    The core crates are:

    • crates/types for chain data types;
    • crates/crypto_provider for signing and verification;
    • crates/execution for state transitions;
    • crates/ordering_fast for fast ordering;
    • crates/consensus_cobalt for Cobalt governance;
    • crates/privacy and crates/privacy_orchard for shielded settlement;
    • crates/storage for persistence;
    • crates/network for validator transport;
    • crates/node for node execution and CLI/RPC surfaces;
    • crates/rpc_sdk for client-facing RPC helpers.

    How The Chain Works¶

    The transparent path is:

    1. a wallet signs a transaction with post-quantum account authorization;
    2. the transaction enters a controlled write path or validator mempool;
    3. a proposer forms a block or batch;
    4. validators verify the proposal and vote;
    5. quorum signatures form a certificate;
    6. the block commits, state roots advance, fees burn, and receipts are written;
    7. clients query tx finality, account state, receipts, and account history through read RPC.

    The privacy path is:

    1. a transparent account signs a funding transfer into the Orchard turnstile;
    2. the Orchard action binds deposit metadata into its authorization domain;
    3. validators verify the Orchard/Halo2 proof and signed envelope;
    4. the pool records output commitments, encrypted outputs, retained roots, and nullifiers;
    5. wallets scan encrypted outputs and build spends from retained witnesses;
    6. disclosure packets can reveal selected facts without exposing spend keys, viewing keys, note seeds, or auth paths.

    The governance path is:

    1. validators publish signed trust views;
    2. the chain checks essential subsets, thresholds, and linkedness;
    3. unsafe trust graphs fail before activation;
    4. Cobalt amendment mechanics ratify valid registry and trust-graph changes;
    5. replay bundles make the amendment history inspectable and reject stale or tampered governance artifacts.

    Launch Boundary¶

    The controlled testnet is an engineering network with project-operated validators. It is built to prove protocol correctness, deterministic replay, validator behavior, RPC behavior, wallet behavior, privacy behavior, and operator runbooks before public launch.

    Public launch adds:

    • independent placement evidence across operators, hosts, jurisdictions, legal domains, and funding domains;
    • longer mixed transparent and shielded soaks;
    • external privacy and implementation review;
    • public write-edge policy and abuse controls;
    • custodian and exchange wallet flows;
    • clearer compliance and selective-disclosure operating policy;
    • independent benchmark packets for throughput claims.

    Those are launch requirements. They do not change what has already been built in the controlled implementation.

    Roadmap From Here¶

    The next work is not to invent a different chain. It is to harden this one:

    1. keep the controlled Cobalt readiness gate green while improving placement evidence;
    2. keep transparent finality inside the current 1-2 second controlled target while extending soak duration;
    3. turn the Orchard/Halo2 path into a cleaner semi-production privacy release with stronger wallet, custodian, audit, and compliance surfaces;
    4. formalize public RPC write-edge policy and abuse controls;
    5. package validator operations so a new operator can launch, inspect, recover, and replay without bespoke help;
    6. publish benchmark packets only when the workload, hardware, validator count, and write-path policy are attached to the number.

    PostFiat exists because the XRP-style authority-validator settlement model is valuable, but the next version of that model needs private settlement, post-quantum authorization, explicit Cobalt governance, fixed supply, no validator inflation, and modern operator evidence from the start.
