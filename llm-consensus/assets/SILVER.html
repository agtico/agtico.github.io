SILVER — Silver

Consensus bullishness: 0.11

What The Business Is

Silver is a globally traded precious and industrial metal, priced mainly through OTC London markets and COMEX futures, with benchmarks such as the LBMA Silver Price. It is not an operating business; the “asset” represents exposure to physical silver inventories, mined supply, recycled metal, futures, and physically backed products such as the iShares Silver Trust, whose stated purpose is to own silver transferred to the trust in exchange for shares. ([lbma.org.uk](https://www.lbma.org.uk/prices-and-data/lbma-silver-price?utm_source=openai))

What It Does

Silver functions both as a monetary/precious-metal asset and as an industrial input. Demand spans investment bars/coins and ETPs, jewelry and silverware, plus electronics, solar PV, EVs, batteries, semiconductors and other electrification uses. Unlike gold, silver is not widely held as a central-bank reserve asset, so its price is more directly tied to industrial cycles and physical availability. ([kiplinger.com](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/etfs/the-best-precious-metals-etfs-to-buy?utm_source=openai))

Physically backed vehicles remain large: SLV’s March 31 fact sheet showed about **491.1 Moz** in trust, while other third-party data around early July showed SLV AUM near **$27.9B**. ([silverinstitute.org](https://silverinstitute.org/global-silver-investment-to-remain-strong-in-2026-against-the-backdrop-of-a-sixth-consecutive-annual-market-deficit/?utm_source=openai))

Supply elasticity remains limited. Recent coverage highlighted that silver supply is constrained because much production is a byproduct of other mining, while 2017–2025 demand rose faster than supply; that supports the deficit narrative if investment or industrial demand surprises higher.
