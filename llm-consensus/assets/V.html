V — VISA INC

Consensus bullishness: 0.83

What The Business Is

Visa Inc. is a global payments technology company, not a bank: it operates card and digital-payment networks that connect consumers, merchants, financial institutions, fintechs, governments, and businesses. Its economics are driven by payment volume, processed transactions, cross-border activity, and value-added services such as fraud/risk tools, tokenization, issuer/acquirer services, consulting, and marketing. In fiscal Q2 2026, management described Visa as a “hyperscaler of payments,” with 5 billion credentials, more than 175 million seller locations, and operations across 200 countries and territories; the company reported net revenue of $11.2 billion, up 17% year over year. ([s1.q4cdn.com](https://s1.q4cdn.com/050606653/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/Q2-2026-Earnings-Release_vF.pdf?utm_source=openai))

What It Does

Visa authorizes, clears, and settles electronic payments and provides adjacent services around security, acceptance, money movement, and data. Its core rails include consumer payments, commercial payments, and Visa Direct, which management said had more than 18 billion endpoints and 3.7 billion Q2 transactions, up 23% year over year in the transcript excerpt. Visa is also expanding into AI-enabled commerce and stablecoin/blockchain connectivity: the excerpt cites more than 160 stablecoin card programs, a $7 billion annual run-rate of stablecoin settlement volume, and nine blockchains enabled for settlement. Value-added services are now a major business line: management said VAS was about 30% of net revenue and grew 27% in constant dollars in Q2. ([fool.com](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/29/visa-v-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/?utm_source=openai))

Recent Highlights (Positive)

Fiscal Q2 2026 was strong: Visa reported 17% net revenue growth, GAAP net income of $6.0 billion, GAAP EPS of $3.14, payments volume growth of 9% in constant dollars, processed transactions growth of 9%, and cross-border volume growth excluding intra-Europe of 11%, according to its April 28 earnings release and transcript excerpt. ([s1.q4cdn.com](https://s1.q4cdn.com/050606653/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/Q2-2026-Earnings-Release_vF.pdf?utm_source=openai))

Visa continued to push into agentic commerce. On June 10, 2026, it announced a strategic collaboration with OpenAI to support secure Visa payments in AI-commerce experiences, using tokenized credentials, permissions, real-time authorization, and fraud monitoring; Visa positioned this as part of its Visa Intelligent Commerce strategy. ([visa.gcs-web.com](https://visa.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/visa-partners-openai-power-next-generation-ai-commerce?utm_source=openai))

Visa also announced AI and stablecoin-related product work around Agent Score, an Agentic Directory, a Large Transaction Model for fraud/authorization performance, and expanded AI-commerce tooling, reinforcing management’s view that AI agents could increase transaction counts and create new micro-transaction use cases. ([pymnts.com](https://www.pymnts.com/visa/2026/visa-launches-ai-and-stablecoin-tools-to-power-agentic-commerce/?utm_source=openai))

A legal overhang eased somewhat when, on June 9, 2026, a U.S. district court granted preliminary approval to Visa and Mastercard’s revised merchant-fee settlement; Visa said the agreement remains subject to final court approval and would provide merchants with more flexibility and acceptance options. ([corporate.visa.com](https://corporate.visa.com/en/sites/visa-perspectives/company-news/visa-statement-mdl-settlement.html?utm_source=openai))

Recent Highlights (Negative)

Regulatory and litigation risk remains material. The June 2026 merchant-fee settlement is only preliminary and still requires final court approval; Reuters reported it as a revised $38 billion settlement with merchants alleging excessive credit-card processing fees, so economics and network rules remain under scrutiny. ([streetinsider.com](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Visa%2C%2BMastercard%2B%2438%2Bbillion%2Bswipe%2Bfee%2Bsettlement%2Bwins%2BUS%2Bjudges%2Bapproval/26623315.html?utm_source=openai))

Visa also continues to face U.S. antitrust pressure in debit. The Department of Justice’s 2024 lawsuit alleged Visa monopolized U.S. debit markets, including claims that it controlled more than 60% of U.S. debit transactions and earned over $7 billion in annual debit-processing fees; while not new within the 90-day window, it remains a key unresolved risk for the business model. ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2024/09/24/visa-antitrust-debit-card-monopoly-lawsuit?utm_source=openai))

The transcript excerpt flagged geographic sensitivity: management said CEMEA payments-volume growth stepped down by about 2.5 points from Q1, primarily due to conflict in the Middle East, and noted the company was closely watching regional impacts. This highlights that cross-border travel, regional instability, and macro shocks can affect volume growth.

Agentic commerce and stablecoins are promising but early. Visa’s OpenAI collaboration and agent-payment tooling may expand the addressable market, but adoption, consumer trust, fraud liability, merchant acceptance, and regulatory treatment are not yet proven at scale; press coverage also notes that AI shopping is still nascent rather than a near-term replacement for existing retail channels. ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/10/visa-chatgpt-agents-commerce?utm_source=openai))
