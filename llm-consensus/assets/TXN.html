TXN — TEXAS INSTRUMENTS INC

Consensus bullishness: 0.07

What The Business Is

Texas Instruments is a Dallas-based semiconductor company focused on **analog** and **embedded processing** chips. Its strategy centers on in-house manufacturing/technology, a broad catalog, direct market channels, and long-lived customer/product positions; in 2025, embedded processing was about 15% of revenue, with the broader business focused on analog and embedded products. ([sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/97476/000009747626000059/txn-20251231.htm?utm_source=openai))

What It Does

TI’s chips help electronic systems **manage power, sense and transmit real-world signals, and provide control/processing** in customer designs. The company serves industrial, automotive, personal electronics, communications equipment, and enterprise/data-center-related markets, with a portfolio of 80,000+ products. ([ti.com](https://www.ti.com/about-ti.html?utm_source=openai)) Its model emphasizes internal 300mm manufacturing, which management says supports competitive lead times, cycle flexibility, and long-term cost structure.

Recent Highlights (Positive)

In Q1 2026, TI reported **$4.83 billion revenue**, **$1.55 billion net income**, and **$1.68 EPS**; the transcript excerpt says revenue rose **9% sequentially** and **19% year over year**, with Analog up **22% YoY** and Embedded Processing up **12% YoY**. ([ti.gcs-web.com](https://ti.gcs-web.com/static-files/b6565bf6-eef3-4a50-a12a-cce6cc24611b?utm_source=openai)) Management highlighted broad recovery signals: industrial revenue increased more than **30% YoY** and more than **20% sequentially**, data center grew about **90% YoY**, and communications equipment grew about **25% YoY**. The company guided Q2 2026 revenue to **$5.0 billion–$5.4 billion** and EPS to **$1.77–$2.05**, implying continued sequential growth from Q1. ([simplywall.st](https://simplywall.st/stocks/us/semiconductors/nasdaq-txn/texas-instruments/news/texas-instruments-txn-is-up-75-after-strong-q2-ai-demand-gui/amp?utm_source=openai))

TI also continued to benefit from U.S. manufacturing investments: the transcript says trailing-12-month free cash flow rose to **$4.4 billion**, including **$965 million of CHIPS Act incentives**, with a **$555 million** payment tied to start of production at its newest Sherman, Texas 300mm wafer fab. The Sherman fab began production before the 90-day window but remains relevant because Q1 cash flow included the incentive payment and management described capacity/inventory as supporting a wide range of upturn scenarios. ([tomshardware.com](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/new-texas-instruments-fab-will-pump-out-tens-of-millions-of-chips-per-day-first-300mm-fab-starts-production-after-usd60-billion-investment?utm_source=openai)) Silicon Labs shareholders also approved TI’s proposed acquisition in late April, moving the $7.5 billion embedded wireless connectivity deal one step forward. Recent Highlights (Negative)

Management repeatedly cautioned that the strong industrial recovery may not yet be fully proven: the transcript notes macro/geopolitical uncertainty, a prior “head-fake” analog recovery pattern, and the need to see whether Q2 demand is sustainable. Automotive was comparatively less robust: Q1 automotive was only mid-single-digit YoY and roughly flat sequentially, with China down while the rest of world was up.

The Silicon Labs transaction adds execution, financing, and regulatory risk. TI’s own deal announcement lists risks including failure to obtain required approvals, disruption to operations and relationships, retention issues, litigation, unexpected costs, and potential deal delays or termination.
