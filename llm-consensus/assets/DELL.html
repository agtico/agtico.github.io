DELL — DELL TECHNOLOGIES INC

Consensus bullishness: 0.03

What The Business Is

Dell Technologies Inc. is a global technology hardware, infrastructure and services company organized around two reportable segments: **Infrastructure Solutions Group (ISG)** and **Client Solutions Group (CSG)**. ISG covers AI-optimized servers, traditional servers and networking, storage, and related services; CSG covers commercial and consumer PCs, workstations, peripherals, software and support services. Dell describes its portfolio as spanning “client devices and peripherals” through servers, networking and storage for the data and AI era. ([sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1571996/000157199626000030/dell-20260501.htm))

What It Does

Dell sells enterprise infrastructure and endpoint products into corporate, cloud, public-sector and consumer markets. In ISG, it provides AI servers for training, fine-tuning and inference; traditional servers for enterprise workloads; networking hardware; and storage products across primary, unstructured, data-protection, all-flash, software-defined and hyperconverged architectures. ([sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1571996/000157199626000030/dell-20260501.htm)) In CSG, Dell sells branded notebooks, desktops, workstations and peripherals, with a heavier mix toward commercial customers and services such as configuration, deployment, support and warranties. ([sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1571996/000157199626000030/dell-20260501.htm))

The current business narrative is heavily tied to AI infrastructure. On the May 28, 2026 fiscal Q1 2027 call, management emphasized the “Dell AI Factory with NVIDIA,” integrated rack-scale systems, PowerEdge servers, storage and data platforms, and on-prem/edge AI deployments for enterprises seeking control over data, security and sovereignty. Dell’s June 2026 announcement added a PowerEdge XE8812 server using NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL4 architecture, designed for HPC and AI workloads and scaling to 144 GPUs per rack. ([investors.delltechnologies.com](https://investors.delltechnologies.com/static-files/b63ffff9-b729-403b-a231-c6af05667759?utm_source=openai))

Recent Highlights (Positive)

Dell reported a record fiscal Q1 2027: revenue of **$43.8 billion**, up **88% year over year**; non-GAAP diluted EPS of **$4.86**, up **214%**; and first-quarter operating cash flow of **$4.1 billion**. ISG revenue reached **$29.0 billion**, up **181%**, while CSG revenue was **$14.6 billion**, up **17%**. ([investors.delltechnologies.com](https://investors.delltechnologies.com/news-releases/news-release-details/dell-technologies-delivers-first-quarter-fiscal-2027-financial?utm_source=openai))

AI server demand was the standout driver. Dell said it booked **$24.4 billion** of AI orders, recognized **$16.1 billion** of AI server revenue, and raised fiscal 2027 AI server revenue expectations to **$60 billion**. Management also cited a record **$51.3 billion** AI backlog and more than **5,000** Dell AI Factory customers globally. ([investors.delltechnologies.com](https://investors.delltechnologies.com/news-releases/news-release-details/dell-technologies-delivers-first-quarter-fiscal-2027-financial?utm_source=openai))

Product-cycle momentum also improved. At Dell Technologies World in May, Dell announced PowerStore Elite, new 18th-generation PowerEdge servers, cyber-resilience and automation updates; PowerStore Elite is positioned as tripling performance and density versus prior generations, while the 18th-gen PowerEdge line targets higher performance and consolidation for modern data centers. ([dell.com](https://www.dell.com/en-us/dt/corporate/newsroom/announcements/detailpage.press-releases~usa~2026~05~dell-technologies-reimagines-the-modern-data-center-for-the-ai-era.htm))

PCs were not only a drag: Dell’s CSG grew in the quarter, and recent Omdia-reported U.S. PC data cited Dell as taking the top U.S. PC share position in Q1 2026 with 25% share, even as the broader market was pressured. ([tomshardware.com](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/u-s-pc-shipments-drop-7-percent-market-isnt-expected-to-bounce-back-until-2029-price-hikes-and-component-shortages-take-hold-as-pc-market-declines-omdia-report-suggests))

Recent Highlights (Negative)

Supply constraints are a central risk to execution. On the May 28 call excerpt, management said demand exceeded supply, with memory the primary constraint, and expected to exit the year with meaningful backlog. A June Supply Chain Dive report similarly described a structural memory crunch, with manufacturers shifting wafer capacity toward high-bandwidth memory for AI infrastructure and reducing conventional server-memory availability. ([investors.delltechnologies.com](https://investors.delltechnologies.com/static-files/b63ffff9-b729-403b-a231-c6af05667759?utm_source=openai))

Gross margin mix remains a watch item. Dell’s fiscal Q1 gross margin rate was **18.1%**, and management attributed the rate pressure primarily to mix shift toward AI servers, while saying AI server profitability was in line with its mid-single-digit operating-income-rate target in the call excerpt.
