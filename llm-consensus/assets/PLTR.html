PLTR — PALANTIR TECHNOLOGIES INC

Consensus bullishness: 0.21

What The Business Is

Palantir Technologies is a software-infrastructure company that builds and deploys “central operating system” platforms for government and commercial customers, originally rooted in U.S. intelligence/counterterrorism work and now spanning defense, healthcare, manufacturing, finance, energy, agriculture, and other data-heavy operations. Its four principal platforms are Gotham, Foundry, Apollo, and AIP, with revenue generated from cloud subscriptions, on-premise software, maintenance/O&M services, and professional services. ([investors.palantir.com](https://investors.palantir.com/files/2026%20Q1%20PLTR%2010-Q.pdf))

What It Does

Palantir’s core value proposition is integrating fragmented enterprise data, decisions, and operations into operational workflows. Foundry is the data-operations and ontology platform; Gotham supports allied defense and intelligence missions; Apollo manages continuous software delivery across cloud, on-prem, and edge environments; and AIP connects large language models and AI agents to enterprise data and operations under security, governance, and audit constraints. ([investors.palantir.com](https://investors.palantir.com/files/2026%20Q1%20PLTR%2010-Q.pdf)) In the May 4, 2026 earnings-call excerpt, management emphasized AIP as a “no-slop zone” for governed agentic AI, with examples across AIG underwriting/claims, GE Aerospace production and military aviation supply chains, mortgage workflows, Maven defense usage, Navy Ship OS, and USDA farm-security modernization.

Recent Highlights (Positive)

Q1 2026 results were exceptionally strong: revenue rose 85% year over year to $1.633 billion, U.S. revenue rose 104% to $1.282 billion, U.S. commercial revenue rose 133% to $595 million, GAAP operating margin reached 46%, adjusted operating margin reached 60%, and Palantir raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $7.650–$7.662 billion, implying about 71% growth. ([sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1321655/000132165526000026/a2026q1ex991pressrelease.htm)) Customer count reached 1,007, up from 769 a year earlier, and customers pay for software-platform access under generally one- to five-year contracts, though some are shorter-term. ([investors.palantir.com](https://investors.palantir.com/files/2026%20Q1%20PLTR%2010-Q.pdf))

In late April, USDA and Palantir announced a $300 million blanket purchase agreement to support the National Farm Security Action Plan, modernize farmer-service delivery, build on USDA’s Landmark platform, and support the “One Farmer, One File” initiative. ([investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/usda-and-palantir-sign-300-million-software-purchase-agreement-4629688)) In late June, NVIDIA said Palantir’s new intelligent engine would use NVIDIA Nemotron open models for U.S. government agencies, with AIP/Ontology/Foundry/Apollo forming a sovereign AI operating layer for sensitive and air-gapped environments. ([blogs.nvidia.com](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/palantir-secure-ai-us-agencies-nemotron-open-models/))

Recent Highlights (Negative)

Political, procurement, and reputational risk remained prominent. In April, UK MPs called for the government to scrap Palantir’s £330 million NHS contract, citing concerns including vendor lock-in, value for money, and data security; the NHS said patient data remained protected and Palantir said processing was under NHS legal and contractual control. ([theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/apr/16/labour-and-lib-dem-mps-demand-shameful-palantir-nhs-contract-be-scrapped)) In May, London mayor Sadiq Khan’s office blocked a planned £50 million Metropolitan Police contract with Palantir, citing a “clear and serious breach” of procurement rules, while Palantir accused Khan of politicizing public safety.
