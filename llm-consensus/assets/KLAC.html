KLAC — KLA CORP

Consensus bullishness: -0.22

What The Business Is

KLA Corp. is a semiconductor equipment and services company focused on process control: inspection, metrology, data analytics and related service support used by chipmakers to improve yield and accelerate process ramps. The company says it provides process-control and process-enabling solutions for wafers and reticles, integrated circuits, packaging and printed circuit boards. ([ir.kla.com](https://ir.kla.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/514/kla-corporation-reports-fiscal-2026-third-quarter-results))

What It Does

KLA’s tools help semiconductor manufacturers detect defects, measure critical dimensions, monitor process variation and improve yield across foundry/logic, memory, advanced packaging and PCB markets. In the March 2026 earnings call excerpt, management emphasized AI-driven demand, leading-edge foundry/logic, high-bandwidth memory and advanced wafer-level packaging as key demand vectors; it also described service revenue as a predictable cash-flow anchor as installed tools become more advanced and remain in fabs longer.

Recent Highlights (Positive)

KLA reported strong March-quarter FY2026 results on April 29: revenue of **$3.415 billion**, above the midpoint of guidance, GAAP EPS of **$9.12**, non-GAAP EPS of **$9.40**, quarterly free cash flow of **$622.3 million**, and quarterly capital returns of **$874.8 million**. Management guided June-quarter revenue to **$3.575 billion ± $200 million** and non-GAAP EPS to **$9.87 ± $1.00**. ([ir.kla.com](https://ir.kla.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/514/kla-corporation-reports-fiscal-2026-third-quarter-results))

Capital returns also increased: at its March 12 Investor Day, KLA announced a new **$7 billion** share-repurchase authorization, on top of $3.944 billion remaining as of Dec. 31, 2025, and raised the quarterly dividend 21% to **$2.30** per share, its 17th consecutive annual dividend increase. ([ir.kla.com](https://ir.kla.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/512/kla-hosts-investor-day-announces-7-billion-share)) On May 7, KLA also approved a **10-for-1 forward stock split**, with split-adjusted trading beginning June 12, 2026, and said the action was intended to improve share accessibility and liquidity. ([d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net](https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_bad6af6a7e63229ced7915b56c3bd6e5/klatencor/news/2026-05-07_KLA_Corporation_Announces_Ten_to_One_Stock_Split_515.pdf))

Operationally, the transcript highlighted share gains in process control and advanced packaging: management said KLA reached the No. 1 position in process control for advanced wafer-level packaging in 2025 and now expects advanced-packaging process-control revenue to rise from about **$635 million in 2025** to about **$1 billion in 2026**. Management also said customer urgency and backlog visibility into 2027 were unusually strong, tied to leading-edge and greenfield fab activity.

Recent Highlights (Negative)

China/export-control risk remains a live overhang. KLA’s own recent filings and press releases continue to flag evolving U.S. Department of Commerce rules, China customer restrictions, tariffs and trade barriers as risks to selling products and services. ([ir.kla.com](https://ir.kla.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/514/kla-corporation-reports-fiscal-2026-third-quarter-results)) Separately, late-April reports said the U.S. Commerce Department directed U.S. semiconductor equipment suppliers, including KLA, to halt certain shipments to China’s Hua Hong/Huali Microelectronics, reflecting tighter controls around advanced chipmaking capacity. ([ajupress.com](https://www.ajupress.com/view/20260429160470346?utm_source=openai)) In the transcript excerpt, management said the Hua Hong-related letter was being reviewed and the near-term impact was “fairly immaterial,” but broader China growth was expected to lag overall wafer-fab-equipment growth.

Margin and supply-chain pressure also remain relevant. In the transcript excerpt, KLA guided calendar-2026 gross margin around **62% ± 50 bps** while noting elevated DRAM costs for image-processing computers embedded in its systems, a roughly **100 bp** gross-margin headwind expected to persist over the next several quarters, along with tariff and mix effects. The business is also cyclical and concentrated in semiconductor capex, so any pullback in foundry/logic, memory, advanced packaging or AI-infrastructure spending could pressure order visibility and revenue growth.
