CSCO — CISCO SYSTEMS INC

Consensus bullishness: -0.34

What The Business Is

Cisco Systems is a large enterprise technology vendor centered on networking infrastructure, with adjacent security, observability, collaboration, cloud-management, compute, and services offerings. It sells hardware, software, subscriptions, and support to enterprises, public-sector customers, telecom/service providers, cloud providers, and partners; since the Splunk acquisition, its story is increasingly framed around secure networking plus data/observability for AI-era operations. ([cisco.com](https://www.cisco.com/site/us/en/about/index.html))

What It Does

Cisco’s core products connect and secure digital infrastructure: campus and data-center switching, routing, wireless, optical networking via Acacia, Silicon One-based systems, industrial IoT, firewalls/SASE/XDR, collaboration devices/Webex, and Splunk-led observability/security analytics. In the May 13, 2026 quarter, product revenue was $12.1B and services revenue was $3.7B; Networking revenue grew 25% year over year, Observability grew 3%, Collaboration declined 1%, and Security was flat. ([newsroom.cisco.com](https://newsroom.cisco.com/c/r/newsroom/en/us/a/y2026/m05/cisco-reports-third-quarter-earnings.html?source=rss))

Recent Highlights (Positive)

Cisco’s Q3 FY2026 results, reported May 13 for the quarter ended April 25, showed record revenue of $15.8B, up 12% year over year, with GAAP EPS up 37% and non-GAAP EPS up 10%; total product orders rose 35%, or 19% excluding hyperscalers. Management also lifted FY2026 guidance to $62.8B–$63.0B of revenue and non-GAAP EPS of $4.27–$4.29. ([newsroom.cisco.com](https://newsroom.cisco.com/c/r/newsroom/en/us/a/y2026/m05/cisco-reports-third-quarter-earnings.html?source=rss))

AI infrastructure demand was the clearest upside theme in the transcript and release: Cisco said hyperscaler AI infrastructure orders reached $5.3B year-to-date and raised expected FY2026 AI infrastructure orders to $9B, while raising expected FY2026 AI infrastructure revenue to $4B. Campus networking orders grew more than 25%, and data-center switching orders grew more than 40%, supporting management’s claim that enterprise AI and campus refresh are driving a multi-year demand cycle. ([newsroom.cisco.com](https://newsroom.cisco.com/c/r/newsroom/en/us/a/y2026/m05/cisco-reports-third-quarter-earnings.html?source=rss))

Cisco continued adding product and M&A pieces around AI security and observability. It announced intent to acquire Galileo on April 9, 2026 for AI observability/trust tooling, and Astrix Security on May 4, 2026 for securing non-human identities such as API keys, service accounts, OAuth tokens, and AI agents. ([cisco.com](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/about/corporate-strategy-office/acquisitions/acquisitions-list-names.html))

At Cisco Live U.S. in June, Cisco emphasized AI-ready data centers, resilient infrastructure, future workplaces, Cisco Cloud Control, Cisco Live Protect, Cisco IQ, agentic security, and next-generation Silicon One capabilities, positioning these as a unified platform response to AI-driven operational complexity. ([newsroom.cisco.com](https://newsroom.cisco.com/c/r/newsroom/en/us/a/y2026/m06/cisco-live-u-s-leading-in-the-agentic-ai-age.html))

Cisco also announced the Universal Quantum Switch on April 23, a working research prototype designed to route and preserve quantum information over standard telecom fiber at room temperature; this is early-stage, but it reinforces Cisco’s long-horizon networking R&D narrative. ([newsroom.cisco.com](https://newsroom.cisco.com/c/r/newsroom/en/us/a/y2026/m04/cisco-introduces-universal-quantum-switch-advancing-the-path-to-a-quantum-network.html))
