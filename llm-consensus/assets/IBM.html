IBM — INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORP

Consensus bullishness: -0.07

What The Business Is

International Business Machines Corp. is a large enterprise technology company focused on hybrid cloud, AI, software, infrastructure and consulting. Management describes IBM as a “software-led hybrid cloud and AI platform company,” with enterprise trust, security, governance and mission-critical workloads as core positioning. In Q1 2026, IBM reported revenue across Software, Consulting, Infrastructure and Financing, with Software the largest segment at about $7.1 billion of quarterly revenue. ([newsroom.ibm.com](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-04-22-IBM-RELEASES-FIRST-QUARTER-RESULTS))

What It Does

IBM sells enabling software and platforms—Red Hat, automation, data, transaction processing, watsonx AI, Confluent data streaming, and mainframe software—alongside Consulting services and Infrastructure products such as IBM Z, LinuxONE, Power and storage. The transcript emphasized that IBM’s software portfolio is mostly “enabling” or infrastructure software rather than end-user applications, and that AI demand can raise consumption of Red Hat, Confluent, automation, data and mainframe capacity as enterprises move AI from experiments into core systems. IBM also continues to invest in quantum computing, announcing a plan to spend more than $10 billion over five years on R&D, capex, manufacturing scale, ecosystem partnerships and M&A. ([prnewswire.com](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ibm-commits-more-than-10-billion-to-quantum-computing-funding-its-roadmap-from-todays-leading-systems-to-the-worlds-first-fault-tolerant-quantum-computers-302788490.html))

Recent Highlights (Positive)

IBM’s Q1 2026 results showed broad growth: Software revenue rose 7.9% in constant currency, Infrastructure rose 11.7%, and free cash flow was $2.22 billion versus $1.96 billion a year earlier. Management maintained full-year expectations for more than 5% constant-currency revenue growth and about $1 billion of year-over-year free-cash-flow growth, while citing a strong start to the year. ([newsroom.ibm.com](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-04-22-IBM-RELEASES-FIRST-QUARTER-RESULTS))

AI and hybrid-cloud product activity remained active. At Think 2026, IBM announced an expanded enterprise AI operating model, including next-generation watsonx Orchestrate as an agentic control plane, IBM Bob as a generally available enterprise development agent, and watsonx.data capabilities tied to Confluent’s real-time data streaming. ([newsroom.ibm.com](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-05-05-think-2026-ibm-delivers-the-blueprint-for-the-ai-operating-model-as-the-ai-divide-widens?asPDF=1)) IBM also announced a Google Cloud partnership and new Google Cloud Practice, described as a multi-billion-dollar services opportunity using IBM Consulting Advantage, Gemini Enterprise, cybersecurity and data capabilities. ([prnewswire.com](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ibm-and-google-cloud-announce-strategic-partnership-to-scale-ai-with-human-expertise-and-aipowered-delivery-302790899.html))

Infrastructure momentum is another positive theme. In the transcript, management said IBM Z grew 48% in Q1, helped by z17 and AI/security use cases. On July 7, IBM announced new z17 and LinuxONE 5 configurations, including rack-mount options for the first time, broadening deployment flexibility for critical workloads. ([newsroom.ibm.com](https://newsroom.ibm.com/press-releases-corporate?l=25&o=0))

Recent Highlights (Negative)

Consulting remains the softest major segment. Q1 Consulting revenue grew only 0.9% in constant currency, and segment margin slipped to 10.6% from 11.0% a year earlier, despite signings growth and management commentary that generative AI represented about 30% of backlog. This suggests AI-related consulting demand has not yet translated into broad acceleration in reported revenue. ([newsroom.ibm.com](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-04-22-IBM-RELEASES-FIRST-QUARTER-RESULTS))

The Confluent acquisition adds integration and balance-sheet execution risk. IBM invested $10.5 billion in acquisitions in Q1, primarily Confluent, and ended the quarter with total debt of $66.4 billion, up $5.1 billion year to date. Management also said Confluent would create about $600 million of 2026 dilution, largely from stock-based compensation and interest expense, although cost synergies are expected to offset some pressure. ([newsroom.ibm.com](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-04-22-IBM-RELEASES-FIRST-QUARTER-RESULTS?asPDF=1&utm_source=openai))

Infrastructure growth may be cyclical rather than linear. While z17 drove a strong Q1, management still expected full-year Infrastructure revenue to decline low single digits, implying the early z17 surge may not persist at the same rate through 2026. IBM also lists macro weakness, client spending pressure, acquisition integration, AI-related risks, cybersecurity, government-client exposure and higher debt among risk factors that could affect results. ([marketbeat.com](https://www.marketbeat.com/earnings/reports/2026-4-22-international-business-machines-co-stock/?utm_source=openai))
