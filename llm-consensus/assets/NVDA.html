NVDA — NVIDIA CORP

Consensus bullishness: 0.56

What The Business Is

NVIDIA is a technology company centered on accelerated computing: GPUs, CPUs, networking, systems, and software used to train and run AI, high-performance computing, graphics, robotics, automotive, and other compute-heavy workloads. Its current business is dominated by AI data-center infrastructure; in Q1 FY2027, NVIDIA reported record revenue of **$81.6B**, up **85% year over year**, with **Data Center revenue of $75.2B**, up **92% year over year**. ([investor.nvidia.com](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-First-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx?utm_source=openai))

What It Does

NVIDIA sells full-stack AI infrastructure: GPU/CPU compute, rack-scale systems such as Blackwell and Vera Rubin, NVLink interconnect, InfiniBand and Spectrum-X Ethernet networking, BlueField DPUs, CUDA software libraries, AI models, and vertical platforms for robotics, automotive, scientific computing, and enterprise AI. In the provided May 20, 2026 transcript, management emphasized that it is reframing reporting around **Data Center** and **Edge Computing**, with Data Center split into **Hyperscale** and **ACIE** — AI clouds, industrial, enterprise, and sovereign AI — to better reflect AI-factory demand across cloud and non-cloud customers.

Recent Highlights (Positive)

NVIDIA’s Q1 FY2027 results showed continued AI-infrastructure acceleration: management reported **$81.6B** revenue, **$61.2B** gross profit, **$53.5B** operating income, and Q2 FY2027 revenue guidance of **$91B ±2%**, while also announcing an **additional $80B share-repurchase authorization** and raising the quarterly dividend to **$0.25/share**. ([nvidianews.nvidia.com](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/_gallery/download_pdf/6a0e17dc3d633295d45282e6/?utm_source=openai)) The transcript excerpt adds that Blackwell systems drove the quarter, with Data Center revenue of roughly **$75B**, hyperscale revenue near **$38B**, and ACIE near **$37B**, suggesting demand breadth beyond the largest cloud platforms.

Product cadence also remained a major positive. NVIDIA said **Vera Rubin** is ramping into full production, supported by hundreds of ecosystem partners across 30 countries, and positioned it as the foundation for next-generation “AI factories” with materially higher agentic-AI throughput versus Grace Blackwell. ([nvidianews.nvidia.com](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/vera-rubin-full-production-agentic-ai-factory?utm_source=openai)) In June, NVIDIA also highlighted Vera Rubin adoption for scientific supercomputing, including systems from Bull, Dell, GIGABYTE, HPE, and Supermicro and deployments tied to major research institutions. ([nvidianews.nvidia.com](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-vera-rubin-delivers-world-class-supercomputers-for-science?utm_source=openai))

Recent Highlights (Negative)

China remains a material uncertainty. In the transcript, management said the U.S. government had approved licenses for H200 shipments to China-based customers, but NVIDIA had **not yet generated revenue** and was **not including China Data Center compute revenue** in its Q2 outlook. Recent reporting also indicates U.S. export-control enforcement has tightened around advanced AI chips and Chinese-parented entities, adding regulatory complexity for Blackwell/Rubin-class shipments. ([investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/us-takes-step-to-halt-nvidia-ai-chip-shipments-to-chinese-firms-outside-china-4717939?utm_source=openai))

The growth profile is increasingly dependent on enormous AI-infrastructure capex, power availability, and supply-chain execution. Management described “AI factories” as constrained by power and capital, and said NVIDIA had raised total supply commitments, including inventory purchase commitments and prepaids, to **$145B** in Q1. That supports demand visibility, but also raises execution and inventory-risk sensitivity if customer buildouts, financing conditions, or AI monetization expectations change.

Competition and customer concentration are also watch items. NVIDIA’s largest demand pool remains hyperscalers and frontier AI labs, while major customers continue to develop internal accelerators and alternative compute stacks. Export limits may also accelerate domestic Chinese alternatives; reports have described regulatory and import frictions that could reduce NVIDIA’s access to what was historically a meaningful China AI-chip opportunity. ([tomshardware.com](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/huawei-could-seize-chinas-ai-chip-crown-in-2026-as-nvidias-h200-shipments-stall-in-regulatory-limbo-beijing-pushes-homegrown-ai-hardware-dominance-in-a-market-projected-to-hit-usd67-billion-by-2030?utm_source=openai))
