What The Business Is

Broadcom Inc. is a large infrastructure-technology company spanning **semiconductors** and **infrastructure software**. Its chip businesses serve AI/data centers, networking, broadband, wireless, storage, industrial and other connectivity markets, while software includes VMware/private cloud, mainframe, cybersecurity, enterprise software and Fibre Channel management. ([broadcom.com](https://www.broadcom.com/?utm_source=openai)) The company’s portfolio has been assembled through major combinations including legacy Broadcom/Avago, LSI, Brocade, CA Technologies, Symantec enterprise security and VMware. ([broadcom.com](https://www.broadcom.com/company/about-us/company-history?utm_source=openai))

What It Does

Broadcom designs and supplies custom and merchant silicon: AI accelerators/XPUs, Ethernet switching and routing silicon, SerDes, optical/DSP components, wireless connectivity, broadband access chips, storage adapters/controllers and industrial components. Its software segment centers on VMware Cloud Foundation and related private-cloud infrastructure, plus CA/Symantec/Brocade software assets. ([broadcom.com](https://www.broadcom.com/products?utm_source=openai)) In the June 3, 2026 transcript excerpt, management emphasized that AI revenue is being driven by custom XPUs/TPUs and AI networking for a small group of hyperscale and frontier-AI customers, while VMware Cloud Foundation is positioned for private cloud and enterprise AI inferencing workloads.

Recent Highlights (Positive)

Broadcom reported **record fiscal Q2 2026 revenue of $22.2 billion**, up **48% year over year**, with record adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow; management attributed the beat primarily to accelerating AI semiconductor demand. ([broadcom.com](https://www.broadcom.com/company/news/financial-releases/64371?utm_source=openai)) AI semiconductor revenue was **$10.8 billion**, up **143% year over year**, and the company guided fiscal Q3 revenue to **$29.4 billion**, with AI semiconductor revenue expected at **$16 billion**, up more than 200% year over year. ([investors.broadcom.com](https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcom-inc-announces-second-quarter-fiscal-year-2026-financial?utm_source=openai))

The transcript excerpt adds significant order-visibility claims: management said Q2 AI semiconductor bookings exceeded **$30 billion**, reiterated fiscal 2026 AI semiconductor revenue of about **$56 billion**, and said fiscal 2027 AI semiconductor revenue should exceed **$100 billion**. It also cited long-term AI relationships with Google, Anthropic, OpenAI and Meta, including multi-generation TPU/XPU and networking arrangements, plus planned deployments measured in gigawatts. In software, Broadcom said infrastructure software revenue was **$7.2 billion** in Q2, up 9% year over year, with ARR growth of 17%, and guided Q3 software revenue to **$8.9 billion**, up 31%.

Recent Highlights (Negative)

The biggest recent overhang remains VMware customer and regulatory friction. In March 2026, CISPE filed an antitrust complaint with the European Commission alleging Broadcom’s VMware licensing and cloud-service-provider program changes were unfair; Broadcom disputed the complaint and argued CISPE misrepresented the market. ([itpro.com](https://www.itpro.com/cloud/virtualisation/cispe-files-antitrust-complaint-over-broadcom-vmware-partner-program-changes?utm_source=openai)) Customer pushback has continued: recent reporting says T-Mobile is moving tens of thousands of virtual machines off VMware amid litigation over support obligations, while Tesco reportedly removed about 40,000 servers from VMware infrastructure after a separate dispute. ([arstechnica.com](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2026/07/t-mobile-moving-tens-of-thousands-of-virtual-machines-off-vmware-amid-lawsuit/?utm_source=openai))

There are also operating and mix risks embedded in the otherwise strong AI ramp. In the transcript excerpt, Broadcom said consolidated gross margin would fall to roughly **74%** in fiscal Q3 from **77.1%** in Q2 as lower-margin AI semiconductor revenue becomes a larger share of mix, though management argued operating margin should remain around 67%. The AI outlook is also customer-concentrated and supply-dependent: management described six core AI customers and said it was securing wafer/HBM-type supply for 2026–2027 while working on 2028–2029, leaving execution, capacity and customer-ramp timing as key variables.
