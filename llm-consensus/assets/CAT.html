What The Business Is

Caterpillar Inc. is a global industrial manufacturer centered on heavy equipment, engines, turbines, locomotives, services, and captive financing. It reports through **Power & Energy**, **Construction Industries**, **Resource Industries**, and **Financial Products**; its 2025 sales and revenues were **$67.6 billion**, and it describes itself as the world’s leading maker of construction/mining equipment, off-highway diesel and natural-gas engines, industrial gas turbines, and diesel-electric locomotives. ([investors.caterpillar.com](https://investors.caterpillar.com/news/news-details/2026/Caterpillar-Inc--Increases-Dividend/default.aspx?utm_source=openai))

What It Does

Caterpillar sells and supports machinery used in nonresidential/infrastructure construction, mining, quarrying, aggregates, oil & gas, industrial applications, and power generation, with distribution through a large independent dealer network and financing through Cat Financial. In the supplied April 30, 2026 Q1 call excerpt, management emphasized three demand pillars: construction activity supported by infrastructure/data-center buildout; mining demand tied to copper/gold and fleet age; and especially power generation for data centers, cloud computing, and generative AI. It also highlighted compact-equipment initiatives and mining-software expansion via RPMGlobal, which Caterpillar acquired in February 2026 to add mine-planning and operations software capabilities. ([caterpillar.com](https://www.caterpillar.com/en/news/corporate-press-releases/h/rpm-global-acquisition.html?utm_source=openai))

Recent Highlights (Positive)

Caterpillar’s **Q1 2026** results were strong: sales and revenues rose **22% year over year to $17.4 billion**, consolidated operating profit rose **20% to $3.1 billion**, and adjusted profit per share was **$5.54** per the transcript excerpt and company release. ([nasdaq.com](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/caterpillar-reports-first-quarter-2026-results-2026-04-30?utm_source=openai)) Management said backlog reached a **record $63 billion**, total Q1 orders were an all-time record, and all three primary segments contributed to backlog growth; it also raised its 2026 sales/revenue outlook to **low double-digit growth** in the call excerpt. Power & Energy remained the standout: the excerpt cited **32%** sales-to-users growth, including **48%** growth in power generation, driven by large generator sets and turbines for data-center applications.

Capital return also remained visible: Caterpillar said on **June 10, 2026** that it raised the quarterly dividend by **8% to $1.63 per share**, payable August 19 to holders of record July 20, and reiterated an expectation to return substantially all MP&E free cash flow over time via dividends and buybacks. ([investors.caterpillar.com](https://investors.caterpillar.com/news/news-details/2026/Caterpillar-Inc--Increases-Dividend/default.aspx?utm_source=openai)) The transcript excerpt also noted **$5.7 billion** returned to shareholders in Q1, including **$5.0 billion** of repurchases, supported by a strong balance sheet and MP&E free cash flow.

Recent Highlights (Negative)

Tariffs remain the clearest near-term pressure point. In the Q1 call excerpt, management said tariffs introduced since the beginning of 2025 cost about **$600 million** in Q1, even after a favorable adjustment versus an earlier **$800 million** estimate; segment margins were materially affected, including roughly **270 bps** in Power & Energy, **550 bps** in Construction Industries, and **500 bps** in Resource Industries. Caterpillar’s Q1 release also flagged trade-policy pressures as a risk factor and discussed tariff impacts in the context of manufacturing costs. There were also pockets of operational softness beneath the headline growth. Management also described a fluid macro backdrop, citing geopolitical uncertainty and elevated energy prices, plus softer Asia/Pacific conditions outside China and anticipated Middle East softening. Finally, the same AI/data-center power story that has helped sentiment may increase execution risk: management is expanding large reciprocating-engine capacity toward nearly **3x 2024 levels**, with incremental investment primarily from **2027–2029**, making order conversion, capacity execution, and service pull-through important items to monitor.
