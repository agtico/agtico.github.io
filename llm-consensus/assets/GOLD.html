GOLD — Gold

Consensus bullishness: 0.18

What The Business Is

Gold is a global monetary and commodity market rather than an operating business. Its value is anchored in above-ground stocks, mine supply, recycling, jewelry/technology demand, investment demand, central-bank reserves, and derivatives/OTC liquidity. The World Gold Council estimates nearly 220,000 tonnes of gold had been mined through end-2025, worth about US$31 trillion, with jewelry, bars/coins, central banks, ETFs, OTC holdings, and industrial uses forming the main stock categories. ([gold.org](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/market-primer/gold-market-primer-market-size-and-structure))

What It Does

Gold functions as jewelry, a reserve asset, a financial hedge, a physical investment product, and a liquid trading instrument. The market is large and highly financialized: WGC estimates gold traded a record US$361 billion per day on average in 2025, with London OTC, COMEX, Shanghai futures, ETFs, bars/coins, and central-bank holdings all contributing to price discovery and liquidity. Mine production typically supplies about 75% of annual supply, while recycling fills the balance and responds faster to price or economic shocks. ([gold.org](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/market-primer/gold-market-primer-market-size-and-structure))

Recent Highlights (Positive)

Central-bank demand remains a key support. WGC’s June 2026 survey said central banks had accumulated an average of 1,000 tonnes of gold annually over the prior four years, versus about 500 tonnes in the preceding decade; 89% of respondents expected global official gold reserves to rise over the next 12 months, and a record 45% expected their own institution’s reserves to increase. ([gold.org](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/central-bank-gold-reserves-survey-2026)) Reported buying also continued: WGC’s July update showed net central-bank purchases of 41 tonnes in May, led by Poland, China, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Singapore, the Czech Republic, and Jordan. ([gold.org](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/gold-focus/2026/07/central-bank-gold-statistics-central-banks-remain-committed-gold))

ETF flows were still positive on a first-half basis despite June weakness. WGC reported that global gold ETF flows were +US$8 billion in H1 2026, with holdings up 18 tonnes to 4,047 tonnes, driven by record first-half inflows in Asia and healthy European demand. ([gold.org](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-etfs-holdings-and-flows/2026/07)) Supply also expanded modestly: Q1 2026 total supply rose 2% year over year to 1,231 tonnes, helped by record first-quarter mine production of 885 tonnes and a 5% increase in recycling to 366 tonnes. ([gold.org](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-q1-2026/supply))

Recent Highlights (Negative)

Near-term investment momentum weakened in June. WGC reported physically backed gold ETFs saw US$8.9 billion of outflows in June, with all regions in redemption and North America losing the most; global gold ETF AUM fell 13% in the month to US$526 billion, and holdings declined by 74 tonnes. WGC attributed North American selling partly to a gold price pullback, hawkish signals from new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, U.S.-Iran conflict-related inflation concerns, rising real-yield expectations, and a stronger dollar, all of which raise gold’s opportunity cost. ([gold.org](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-etfs-holdings-and-flows/2026/07))

Regulatory and market-integrity scrutiny is also rising. On June 10, 2026, LBMA launched a public consultation on Responsible Gold Guidance version 10, aimed at clarifying requirements, incorporating emerging responsible-sourcing expectations, and strengthening implementation for London Good Delivery refiners, with particular focus on due diligence for secondary gold. ([lbma.org.uk](https://www.lbma.org.uk/articles/public-consultation-launch-responsible-gold-guidance-version-10)) Supply growth can also cap scarcity narratives in the short run: Q1 2026 mine production reached an all-time first-quarter high, while recycling increased with high prices, adding incremental physical availability to the market. ([gold.org](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-q1-2026/supply))
