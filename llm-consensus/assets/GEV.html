GEV — GE VERNOVA INC

Consensus bullishness: 0.02

What The Business Is

GE Vernova is a global energy-equipment and services company, not a regulated electric utility: it sells power-generation, grid, electrification, software, and wind technologies to utilities, developers, industrials, governments, and data-center-linked customers. The company describes itself as a “purpose-built global energy company” organized around **Power, Electrification, and Wind**, with roughly 85,000 employees across about 100 countries. ([gevernova.com](https://www.gevernova.com/news/taxonomy/term/9211))

What It Does

The **Power** segment includes gas, nuclear, and hydro equipment and services; in Q1 2026, Power revenue was $5.0B, with Gas Power the largest contributor. **Electrification** sells grid infrastructure such as transformers, switchgear, HVDC, substations, power conversion/storage, and grid software; Q1 Electrification backlog reached $42.4B. **Wind** sells and services onshore/offshore turbines, but remains the weakest segment financially. ([gevernova.com](https://www.gevernova.com/sites/default/files/gev_webcast_10-q_04222026.pdf))

Recent Highlights (Positive)

Q1 2026 was a strong demand/cash quarter: orders were **$18.3B**, up **71% organically**, backlog rose **$13.0B sequentially** to about **$163B**, free cash flow was **$4.8B**, and management raised 2026 guidance to revenue of **$44.5B–$45.5B**, adjusted EBITDA margin of **12%–14%**, and free cash flow of **$6.5B–$7.5B**. The transcript also emphasized that total backlog is now expected to reach **$200B in 2027**, a year earlier than prior expectations. ([gevernova.com](https://www.gevernova.com/sites/default/files/gev_webcast_pressrelease_04222026.pdf))

Power demand remained a central positive. GE Vernova said Gas Power backlog plus slot-reservation agreements rose to **100 GW** in Q1, and in June it booked a Q2 order to supply **two 9HA.02 gas turbines and two H78 generators** for EVN’s Quang Trach II LNG plant in Vietnam, a project expected to produce more than **1.6 GW** by 2030. ([gevernova.com](https://www.gevernova.com/sites/default/files/gev_webcast_pressrelease_04222026.pdf))

Electrification momentum also strengthened. Management said Q1 Electrification equipment orders for data centers were **$2.4B**, more than full-year 2025, while the Prolec GE transformer acquisition added scale and backlog. GE Vernova also launched **GridOS for Transmission** in June, positioning grid software as a response to surging load, renewable integration, and faster grid operations. ([gevernova.com](https://www.gevernova.com/sites/default/files/gev_webcast_pressrelease_04222026.pdf))

Nuclear/SMR optionality remains a visible growth theme: the U.S. and Japan announced up to **$40B** for GE Vernova Hitachi BWRX-300 SMRs in Tennessee and Alabama, with a stated **3 GW** capacity target. GE Vernova Hitachi also expanded a European BWRX-300 supplier collaboration with Velan in June. ([commerce.gov](https://www.commerce.gov/news/fact-sheets/2026/03/fact-sheet-new-energy-projects-us-japan-trade-deal))

Wind remains the main operational drag. In Q1 2026, Wind revenue fell **25% organically**, segment EBITDA loss widened to **$(382)M**, and management attributed the decline to lower onshore equipment deliveries, tariff impacts, and higher offshore contract losses; 2026 Wind guidance still called for about **$400M** of segment EBITDA losses. Offshore Wind legal/project risk stayed in focus. In April, Vineyard Wind sued GE Renewables/GE Vernova-related entities after a contract-payment dispute tied to the 2024 blade failure; AP reported GE
