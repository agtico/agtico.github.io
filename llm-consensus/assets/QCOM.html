QCOM — QUALCOMM INC

Consensus bullishness: 0.35

What The Business Is

QUALCOMM INC (QCOM) is a semiconductor and wireless-technology company best known for Snapdragon chipsets and cellular intellectual property. It operates primarily through **QCT**, which sells chips and platforms for handsets, automotive, IoT, PCs, edge AI and emerging data-center products, and **QTL**, which licenses Qualcomm’s wireless patent portfolio. In its fiscal Q2 2026 call, management framed Qualcomm as a “connected edge to data center” AI compute company, not only a smartphone supplier.

What It Does

Qualcomm designs system-on-chips, modems, RF front-end, connectivity, AI accelerators, CPUs and software platforms used in smartphones, vehicles, PCs, wearables, industrial devices and networks. Its Snapdragon Digital Chassis targets cockpit, telematics, ADAS and automated-driving workloads; Snapdragon X targets AI PCs; industrial/robotics platforms target edge and “physical AI”; and the new Dragonfly portfolio targets data-center CPUs, inference accelerators, connectivity and custom silicon. At Investor Day 2026, Qualcomm said its Dragonfly data-center roadmap includes the C1000 CPU, High Bandwidth Compute, AI300 inference accelerator and custom silicon solutions. ([qualcomm.com](https://www.qualcomm.com/news/releases/2026/06/qualcomm-unveils-comprehensive-data-center-roadmap-for-the-agent?utm_source=openai))

Recent Highlights (Positive)

Fiscal Q2 2026 results were solid versus guidance: Qualcomm reported **$10.6 billion** revenue and **$2.65 non-GAAP EPS**, with QCT revenue of **$9.1 billion** and QTL revenue of **$1.4 billion**; it also returned **$3.7 billion** to shareholders through buybacks and dividends. Automotive remained a standout: QCT Automotive revenue was **$1.3 billion**, up **38% year over year**, and management said automotive annualized revenue exceeded **$5 billion** for the first time, with an expected fiscal 2026 exit run-rate above **$6 billion**. ([s204.q4cdn.com](https://s204.q4cdn.com/645488518/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/FY2026-2nd-Quarter-Earnings-Release.pdf?utm_source=openai))

The biggest recent strategic development was the June 24 Investor Day, where Qualcomm raised its fiscal 2029 **non-handset revenue target to $40 billion**, including **more than $15 billion** from data-center AI infrastructure and **$10 billion** from automotive; it also expanded its automotive design-win pipeline to **$65 billion** and targeted **more than $18 non-GAAP EPS** in fiscal 2029. ([investor.qualcomm.com](https://investor.qualcomm.com/news-events/press-releases/news-details/2026/Qualcomm-Accelerates-Diversification-with-Comprehensive-Strategy-for-Data-Center-and-Sees-Multiple-Inflection-Points-Over-the-Next-3-to-5-Years/default.aspx?utm_source=openai))

Qualcomm also announced a **multi-year, multi-generation agreement with Meta** to supply data-center CPUs, giving more concrete customer validation to its data-center ambitions. The company said the Dragonfly roadmap is optimized for agentic AI inference with emphasis on performance per watt, token throughput and lower total cost of ownership. ([qualcomm.com](https://www.qualcomm.com/news/releases/2026/06/qualcomm-unveils-comprehensive-data-center-roadmap-for-the-agent?utm_source=openai))

Recent Highlights (Negative)

Near-term handset weakness remains the main overhang. On the fiscal Q2 call, management said China Android QCT shipments were “meaningfully below” end-consumer demand because handset OEMs were reducing build plans and drawing down channel inventory amid memory supply and pricing pressures. Qualcomm guided fiscal Q3 revenue to **$9.2 billion–$10.0 billion** and non-GAAP EPS to **$2.10–$2.30**, with QCT handset revenue expected around **$4.9 billion**, down sequentially. ([s204.q4cdn.com](https://s204.q4cdn.com/645488518/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/FY2026-2nd-Quarter-Earnings-Release.pdf?utm_source=openai))

The diversification story is promising but execution-heavy. Data center pits Qualcomm against entrenched AI infrastructure suppliers and hyperscaler in-house silicon programs; its new Investor Day targets are forward-looking and depend on successful product ramps, software ecosystem adoption, customer wins and manufacturing execution. Qualcomm’s own Investor Day release emphasized that targets and diversification initiatives are subject to risks and uncertainties. ([investor.qualcomm.com](https://investor.qualcomm.com/news-events/press-releases/news-details/2026/Qualcomm-Accelerates-Diversification-with-Comprehensive-Strategy-for-Data-Center-and-Sees-Multiple-Inflection-Points-Over-the-Next-3-to-5-Years/default.aspx?utm_source=openai))

China exposure and export constraints add risk. Recent reporting said Qualcomm plans China-compliant versions of Dragonfly data-center products, but that strategy must navigate U.S. export thresholds and a changing regulatory environment.
