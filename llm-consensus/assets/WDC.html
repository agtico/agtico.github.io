WDC — WESTERN DIGITAL CORP

Consensus bullishness: -0.07

What The Business Is

Western Digital Corp. is now primarily a **hard-disk-drive storage infrastructure company** after completing the separation of its Flash business into Sandisk on **February 21, 2025**; WD’s latest filing presentation states Sandisk results are no longer consolidated after that date. ([investor.wdc.com](https://investor.wdc.com/node/28206/pdf)) Its business is increasingly centered on **cloud, hyperscale, enterprise, and AI-driven data storage**, with management describing WD as a partner to hyperscalers and cloud providers in the AI data economy. ([investor.wdc.com](https://investor.wdc.com/node/28206/pdf))

What It Does

WD designs and sells HDDs and related storage technologies used to store large volumes of data persistently and cost-efficiently. In the April 30, 2026 transcript, management emphasized high-capacity nearline HDDs, UltraSMR, ePMR, HAMR, high-bandwidth drive technology, and dual-pivot designs for AI workloads. The company says its roadmap includes **40TB UltraSMR ePMR drives in qualification**, **44TB HAMR qualification**, and a path beyond **100TB** HDDs; its February Innovation Day materials said 40TB UltraSMR ePMR was in qualification with hyperscale customers, volume production was planned for the second half of 2026, and HAMR would scale toward 100TB by 2029. ([investor.wdc.com](https://investor.wdc.com/node/27841/pdf))

Recent Highlights (Positive)

WD’s fiscal Q3 2026 results, reported **April 30, 2026**, were materially stronger year over year: revenue was **$3.34 billion, up 45%**, non-GAAP gross margin was **50.5%**, and non-GAAP diluted EPS was **$2.72**, up **97%**. ([investor.wdc.com](https://investor.wdc.com/node/28206/pdf)) The transcript attributes the strength to cloud demand, better pricing, higher-capacity mix, UltraSMR adoption, and AI-related storage needs from training, inference, agentic AI, synthetic data, and physical AI. Cloud was the dominant segment in the excerpt, at about **$3.0 billion / 89% of revenue**, up **48%** year over year.

The company also strengthened its balance sheet and capital-return profile: Q3 operating cash flow was **$1.12 billion**, free cash flow was **$978 million**, and WD declared a **20% dividend increase** to **$0.15 per share**, payable June 17, 2026 to holders of record June 5, 2026. ([investor.wdc.com](https://investor.wdc.com/node/28206/pdf)) Management also guided fiscal Q4 2026 revenue to **$3.65 billion ± $100 million**, non-GAAP gross margin to **51%–52%**, and non-GAAP EPS to **$3.25 ± $0.15**, implying continued momentum. ([investor.wdc.com](https://investor.wdc.com/node/28206/pdf))

Recent Highlights (Negative)

The strongest positives also create concentration and execution risks. WD’s current profile is heavily tied to cloud and hyperscale AI infrastructure demand; if customer AI storage buildouts, long-term agreements, or data-retention assumptions prove too aggressive, the revenue and pricing environment could normalize. Management’s own forward-looking risk language highlights volatility in demand, competitive products and pricing, dependence on limited qualified suppliers, customer-relationship changes, tariffs/trade restrictions, manufacturing or supply-chain disruptions, debt obligations, cybersecurity risks, and legal/regulatory risks. ([investor.wdc.com](https://investor.wdc.com/node/28206/pdf))

Technology transition risk is also important: the thesis depends on timely qualification and ramp of next-generation ePMR, UltraSMR, HAMR, high-bandwidth, and dual-pivot products. WD said 40TB UltraSMR ePMR volume production is planned for the second half of 2026 and HAMR ramp production in 2027, but qualification delays, yield issues, or customer adoption friction could affect growth and margins. ([investor.wdc.com](https://investor.wdc.com/node/27841/pdf)) In the transcript, operating expenses rose sequentially partly because of accelerated R&D spending as HAMR qualifications expanded, underscoring that the roadmap requires ongoing investment before full commercialization.
