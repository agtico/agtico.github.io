NOW — SERVICENOW INC

Consensus bullishness: 0.91

What The Business Is

ServiceNow is a large-cap enterprise software company whose core platform digitizes and automates workflows across IT, employee services, customer operations, security, risk, finance, procurement, and app development. Management positions the company as an “AI control tower” for enterprise work, with a stated total addressable market above $600 billion and Q1 2026 remaining performance obligations of about $27.7 billion, up 23.5% year over year in constant currency. ([investor.servicenow.com](https://investor.servicenow.com/news/news-details/2026/ServiceNow-Reports-First-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx?utm_source=openai))

What It Does

The Now Platform acts as a system of action that connects data, workflow, AI models, and governance across enterprise departments. Its roots are in IT service management—incident, change, problem, and request management—but the platform now spans IT, HR, customer service, security, risk/compliance, CRM, and app development workflows. ([servicenow.com](https://www.servicenow.com/products/itsm.html?utm_source=openai)) In recent messaging, ServiceNow has emphasized AI-native packaging, autonomous workflow execution, AI specialists, AI Control Tower, Workflow Data Fabric, and EmployeeWorks, combining Moveworks’ conversational AI with ServiceNow’s employee experience capabilities. ([newsroom.servicenow.com](https://newsroom.servicenow.com/press-releases/details/2026/ServiceNow-moves-beyond-the-sidecar-AI-era-giving-customers-a-complete-AI-native-experience-across-all-products-and-packages/default.aspx?utm_source=openai))

Recent Highlights (Positive)

Q1 2026 results beat company guidance across top-line and profitability metrics: subscription revenue was $3.671 billion, up 22% reported and 19% constant currency; cRPO was $12.64 billion, up 21% constant currency; non-GAAP operating margin was 32%; and free cash flow margin was 44%. Management also raised full-year 2026 subscription revenue guidance to $15.735–$15.775 billion, including an estimated 125-basis-point contribution from Armis. ([investor.servicenow.com](https://investor.servicenow.com/news/news-details/2026/ServiceNow-Reports-First-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx?utm_source=openai))

The transcript excerpt highlighted broad AI and large-deal momentum: Now Assist was described as tracking above the company’s 2026 target, customers spending $1 million-plus on Now Assist grew more than 130% year over year, sales CRM net-new ACV grew more than 5x, and the company reported 16 Q1 deals above $5 million in net-new ACV, including five above $10 million. ServiceNow also said 50% of net-new business now comes from non-seat-based pricing, including tokens and infrastructure-style metrics.

Strategically, ServiceNow completed the Armis acquisition on April 20, 2026, following the March 2026 close of Veza, expanding its security-and-risk offering into connected-asset visibility and identity governance. The company then launched Autonomous Security & Risk at Knowledge 2026, integrating Armis and Veza into a broader AI-agent, identity, and asset-governance stack. ([investor.servicenow.com](https://investor.servicenow.com/news/news-details/2026/ServiceNow-completes-Armis-acquisition-closing-the-gap-between-asset-visibility-and-cyber-risk/default.aspx?utm_source=openai))

At Knowledge 2026 and Financial Analyst Day, ServiceNow expanded its AI narrative with Autonomous Workforce, new AI specialists for IT/CRM/employee/security workflows, Action Fabric for third-party AI agents, and broader AI Control Tower capabilities to discover, observe, govern, secure, and measure AI across enterprise systems. ([newsroom.servicenow.com](https://newsroom.servicenow.com/press-releases/details/2026/ServiceNow-turns-enterprise-AI-chaos-into-control-with-the-platform-for-governed-autonomous-work/default.aspx?utm_source=openai))

Recent Highlights (Negative)

Despite the Q1 beat, the stock fell sharply after earnings; Kiplinger reported NOW declined 17.8%, its biggest one-day drop on record, as investors focused on smaller beat magnitude, conservative Q2 cRPO guidance, geopolitical deal-timing headwinds, and acquisition integration expenses. Management cited a roughly 75-basis-point Q1 subscription-revenue headwind from delayed large on-premise deals in the Middle East, and guidance was framed as prudent given ongoing regional conflict and potential deal-timing risk. The company also acknowledged near-term margin headwinds from integrating Armis, with 2026 guidance including estimated pressure to subscription gross margin and operating margin. The acquisition strategy adds execution risk. Armis was reported as a roughly $7.75 billion deal, following the Veza close in March, creating a rapid security-focused expansion that must be integrated into the platform, go-to-market motion, and margin model. ([investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/servicenow-completes-775-billion-armis-acquisition-93CH-4624538?utm_source=openai))

A broader market concern remains whether generative AI and agentic tools disrupt parts of workflow software demand or compress software budgets. ServiceNow’s response is to position itself as the governed execution layer for any model or agent, but the negative side of the consensus debate is that AI could also enable customers or competing platforms to automate workflows without relying as heavily on traditional SaaS seats.
