What The Business Is

Bitcoin is not a company; it is a decentralized monetary network and bearer digital asset. BTC is the native unit used to transfer value, pay transaction fees, and settle ownership on a public blockchain without a central issuer. As of July 8, 2026, BTC trades around **$61,807**, with an intraday range of roughly **$61,630–$64,151**.

What It Does

Bitcoin provides scarce, programmable settlement: users broadcast transactions, miners order them into blocks, and the protocol issues new BTC according to a fixed schedule capped at 21 million coins. Demand now comes from several channels: direct holders, exchanges, miners, corporate/treasury users, derivatives markets, and U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs. ETF creations/redemptions are a major observable demand channel because spot ETF inflows generally require issuers or authorized participants to source BTC, while outflows can release BTC back to market. Current ETF trackers show daily U.S. spot Bitcoin ETF flow data through July 7–8, 2026, with total historical net inflows still large but 2026 year-to-date flows negative on some aggregators. ([walletpilot.com](https://www.walletpilot.com/bitcoin-tracker/etfs?utm_source=openai))

([sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2026/03/s7-2026-09?utm_source=openai)) Earlier in March, the SEC and CFTC announced an interagency MOU to coordinate market oversight and support a more harmonized framework for crypto assets and emerging technologies.
