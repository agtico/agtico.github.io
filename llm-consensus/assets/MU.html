What The Business Is

Micron Technology Inc. (MU) is a U.S.-based semiconductor memory and storage company focused on DRAM, NAND flash, NOR, high-bandwidth memory (HBM), SSDs, and related advanced packaging. Its business is highly cyclical but is currently being reshaped by AI infrastructure demand, where memory bandwidth, capacity, power efficiency, and supply assurance are becoming strategic constraints for cloud, accelerator, automotive, and edge-AI customers. Micron’s fiscal Q3 2026 call emphasized that AI has elevated memory from a commodity input toward a strategic asset, with the company citing tight DRAM and NAND supply conditions expected to persist beyond calendar 2027. ([investors.micron.com](https://investors.micron.com/node/50671/pdf?utm_source=openai))

What It Does

Micron manufactures and sells DRAM, NAND, HBM, data-center SSDs, mobile/client memory, automotive memory, embedded storage, and legacy products for industrial, medical, aerospace, and defense markets. In AI servers, its key growth products include HBM, high-capacity DDR, low-power server DRAM, PCIe Gen6 SSDs, and SOCAMM2. In March 2026, Micron said its HBM4 36GB 12-high product designed for NVIDIA Vera Rubin, along with SOCAMM2 and PCIe Gen6 SSDs, had entered high-volume production. ([investors.micron.com](https://investors.micron.com/news-releases/news-release-details/micron-high-volume-production-hbm4-designed-nvidia-vera-rubin?utm_source=openai))

Recent Highlights (Positive)

Micron reported record fiscal Q3 2026 results on June 24, 2026: revenue of $41.5 billion, record gross margin of 84.9%, and non-GAAP diluted EPS of $25.11; it guided fiscal Q4 revenue to about $50 billion, gross margin to roughly 86%, and EPS to about $31. The company said DRAM revenue reached $31.3 billion and NAND revenue $9.9 billion, both records, supported mainly by sharply higher pricing and favorable mix. ([investors.micron.com](https://investors.micron.com/node/50671/pdf?utm_source=openai))

A major strategic development was Micron’s disclosure of 16 signed strategic customer agreements across data center, consumer, automotive, and other markets. Management said 14 of the agreements represented about $100 billion of remaining performance obligations at minimum contracted price levels, with projected deposits and related commitments of about $22 billion, mostly cash deposits. The company framed these take-or-pay structures as a way to improve revenue visibility, margin durability, and customer supply assurance through the AI-driven shortage cycle. ([investors.micron.com](https://investors.micron.com/static-files/631b1a32-5537-46ae-8f40-82e42fc79dfe?utm_source=openai))

AI memory demand remains a powerful external tailwind.
